SLIDE 3: Members use digital first, then call
============================================================

Talking points:
  - Talk time follows call reason and MSR platform, not digital use.
  - 55.4% of friction calls follow digital use.
  - Five issues are 12.2% of those calls.
  - Fraud blocks: up to about 720K calls a year addressable.
  - Wallet: 93% never tap Add to Wallet.

Script:
Five things to take away. First, talk time is driven by the call reason and by the platform the MSR uses, not by whether the member used the app first. Fourth, for fraud blocks, members cannot see or clear the block in the app, and up to about 720 thousand calls a year end with the MSR simply removing it. Fifth, for wallet verification, the process sends members to the phone: 93 percent never tap Add to Wallet in our app.

SLIDE 7: OneView-only calls are shorter in all 17 categories

Talking points:

  - Both platforms 5.3, UAD only 5.2.
  - Gap holds in all 17 categories and inside the top themes.
  - UAD-only calls transfer 23.6% versus about 9 to 10%.

Script:
This is the new finding. When the MSR handles the call in OneView alone, the median talk time is 3.1 minutes. When UAD is involved, either alone or alongside OneView, it is a little over 5 minutes. That gap of about 2 minutes shows up in every one of the 17 categories, and inside each of the top themes, so it is not explained by call mix. The second signal is transfers: UAD-only calls are transferred 23.6 percent of the time, more than twice the rate of the others. One caution. This is an association. The calls an MSR can finish in OneView alone may simply be the easier ones. The same-MSR comparison is the next check, and I would not call this a tool effect until that is done.

SLIDE 14: How a fraud block turns into a call
============================================================

Talking points:
  - The system blocks the whole card.
  - Member usually finds out at checkout.
  - Only self-serve path is replying to the alert.
  - The app shows no block.
  - The MSR does the same 'Was this you?' check the alert asks for.

Script:
Here is how a fraud block becomes a call. The system flags a purchase and blocks the whole card. The member usually finds out when the card is declined; over half describe a decline. The only self-service path is replying to the alert, if it arrived. The app shows no block and offers no way to clear it. So they call. On the call, in at least 57 percent of cases, the MSR verifies identity, reads back the last 48 hours of charges, asks 'was this you', and removes the block in two to three minutes. That is the key point: the MSR is doing the same check the alert asks for. When the alert misses, the phone is the only way to finish it.

SLIDE 17: Six gaps, one fix: “Was this you?” in the app
============================================================

Talking points:
  - Six gaps, all pointing to one fix.
  - Put the 'Was this you?' review in the app.
  - At least 57% of calls end with the MSR removing the block.
  - Up to about 720K calls a year, an upper bound.
  - Fixes are proposals to validate.

Script:
Putting it together, there are six places members get stuck, and they point to one fix. The block is invisible in the app. Members find out at checkout. The alert misses or does not complete. Freeze is the wrong control. Some are locked out. And Contact Us is the only exit. If the app showed the block and let members confirm their own charges, most of these go away. On sizing: at least 57 percent of these calls end with the MSR removing the block. Applied to 1.26 million calls a year, that is up to about 720 thousand calls. That is an upper bound, and the fixes are proposals we would validate with the fraud and product teams.

SLIDE 19: How members add a card, and where it breaks
============================================================

Talking points:
  - Two ways to add a card.
  - Route A from our app works with no call.
  - Most members use Route B, directly in Wallet.
  - Wallet tells about 95% to call.
  - The in-app verify banner only shows at a fresh sign-in.

Script:
There are two ways to add a card to a wallet. Route A starts in our app. The member is already signed in, the card is approved, and there is no call. Only 6.8 percent of Apple Pay callers started there. Route B is adding the card directly in Apple or Google Wallet. Wallet asks the member to verify, and for about 95 percent of callers who describe it, the only option shown was to call. If they do open our app, the verify banner only appears at a fresh sign-in. On the call, the MSR approves manually in about two minutes at least half the time. So there are three break points: Wallet says call, our verify step is hidden behind sign-in, and new cards push members to Route B. [Add the app screenshot before presenting.]

SLIDE 23: Six gaps: verification sits outside the app
============================================================

Talking points:
  - Six gaps: verification sits outside the app.
  - Restore the app option in the Wallet prompt.
  - Show the verify banner in any session.
  - 895K calls a year at 2.1 minutes.
  - Highest repeat index of the top five.

Script:
The six gaps for wallet. Wallet says call. Members do not know the app can verify. The banner only shows at a fresh sign-in. A success message is not the end of the flow. New and digital cards push members to Wallet. And in-app verification sometimes fails. The fixes are to restore the app option in the Wallet prompt, push a verify link when a card is pending, and show the banner in any session and on the Cards page. This issue is about 895 thousand calls a year across all calls, at a 2.1 minute median, with the highest repeat rate of our top five. As with fraud, these fixes are proposals to validate with the wallet and product teams.

SLIDE 24: Where to act first
============================================================

Talking points:
  - One: in-app 'Was this you?' review, up to about 720K calls a year.
  - Two: verify wallet cards in the app, 895K calls a year.
  - Three: faster path for locked-out members.
  - Next: full-table trend and same-MSR platform check.

Script:
Three places to act first. One, an in-app 'Was this you?' review for fraud blocks, which could address up to about 720 thousand calls a year. Two, make wallet verification visible and reachable in the app, which touches about 895 thousand short calls a year and our highest repeat rate. Three, a faster path for members who are locked out during a fraud block: they are 10 percent of those calls, average over 7 minutes, and 31 percent are transferred. Two pieces of analysis remain. The talk-time trend needs the full calls table, and the platform finding needs the same-MSR check before we treat it as a tool effect. The ask today is agreement to take the first two to the product, fraud and wallet teams for validation.
